# Findings

What the optimisation campaign on the C++ timetabling solver established, organised as **claims with
their evidence** rather than as a chronology. The chronology — every experiment in the order it was
run, including the ones that went wrong and why — is [`CAMPAIGN-LOG.md`](CAMPAIGN-LOG.md). The
catalogue of ideas and their specifications is [`OPTIMIZATION-IDEAS.md`](OPTIMIZATION-IDEAS.md); the
staged testing procedure is [`EXPERIMENT-PROTOCOL.md`](EXPERIMENT-PROTOCOL.md); the stuck-state
design is [`STAGNATION-ESCAPE.md`](STAGNATION-ESCAPE.md).

This file exists for two readers. One is a later agent continuing the optimisation, who needs to know
what is settled so as not to re-derive it. The other is the author of a scientific paper, who needs
the claims separated from the narrative, the statistics attached to each, and the threats to validity
stated honestly. Nothing here is written to flatter the result; several of the most useful findings
are negative, and one of them is the refutation of a rule this campaign itself proposed.

**Corpus.** 1 219 solver runs, ≈ 148 core-hours, on n = 400 … 31 000 classes, at budgets of 10 s to
1 h, all scored by the independent validator `timetable-bench/validate.mjs` rather than by the
solver's own arithmetic.

## 1. The system, in one page

The solver is a multi-threaded C++20 local search for the university course timetabling problem
(UCTP) as the LNU faculty schedules it. Formally: assign each of n classes a (day, start time,
week-parity, room) subject to hard domain restrictions, minimising

    f(σ) = Σᵢ₌₁⁹ βᵢ · Πᵢ(σ)^α,   α = 2,
    β = (150, 100, 50, 90, 120, 50, 5, 20, 30).

Π₁–Π₃ are lecturer, group and room clashes; Π₄–Π₅ are travel-time violations between consecutive
classes in different buildings; Π₆ is abstract-room overflow; Π₇–Π₈ are **вікна** (idle пари between
an entity's first and last class of a day) for lecturers and groups; Π₉ is mixed online/in-person
days for a group. Π₁–Π₆ are treated as hard, Π₇–Π₉ as comfort.

Architecturally: an incremental evaluator over (entity, day) buckets with 128-bit tick masks, so the
cost of a candidate depends on how crowded the touched days are and **not on n**; late-acceptance
hill climbing (ℓ = 100) as the acceptance rule; seven neighbourhoods (move, swap, ejection chain,
Kempe, ruin-and-recreate with five victim selectors, an exact Hungarian permutation, and a day
re-pack) selected by a bandit on reward per unit of work; and a three-level escape — deep phase,
kick from the incumbent, fresh restart at 1.2 M moves without improvement.

The incumbent is chosen lexicographically by `(unplaced, hard, f)`; the acceptance cost prices hard
violations at a finite λ = max(10⁶, 0.02·f̃₀) so the walk can cross infeasible ground.

**Baseline performance.** Always hard = 0 at these sizes; the residual is comfort only, and it
depends on the set and the configuration:

| set | n | budget | workers | median soft | composition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| archived | 12 288 | 1 h | 2 | ≈ 8 (STUDY.md §6) | ~5 lecturer + ~3 group windows |
| tight-XL | 12 800 | 1 h | 1 | 17 (this campaign, C1) | 13–14 lecturer + 3–5 group windows |
| archived | 12 288 | 300 s | 2 | 10 | — |
| tight-XL | 12 800 | 300 s | 2 | 22 | — |

That residual — a dozen or two idle пари among twelve thousand classes — is what the whole campaign
is about.

**Provenance.** Figures attributed to `STUDY.md` predate this campaign and were not re-derived here;
everything else was recomputed from `bench/results/*.jsonl`, which ships with the campaign archive.
A paper should re-verify the `STUDY.md` figures against their own source.

## 2. Method

The apparatus is worth describing separately, because two parts of it are reusable and one of them is
the reason the campaign's negative results can be trusted.

### 2.1 Backwards instance generation

Instances are generated by **planting a feasible schedule first and deriving the instance from it**:
draw a complete timetable, then read off each class's domain, each lecturer's and group's
availability, and each room's rules so that the planted schedule satisfies all of them. The archive
carries the planted schedule as `hidden`.

Two properties follow, and both matter:

- **A perfect answer is known to exist.** A run that ends at soft = 20 is 20 above a *reachable*
  optimum, not 20 above an unknown bound. Without this the whole campaign would be measuring
  differences between arms with no scale to put them on.
- **Instance properties can be varied one at a time.** Room count, room slack, the number of rooms
  each class names, the share of classes naming no room, faculty count and the degree of
  cross-faculty coupling are all generator parameters, and levels of one can be produced holding the
  planted schedule byte-identical. §4 is entirely built on this.

The generator (`bench/build-uni.mjs`, `emit-uni.mjs`, `generate-uni.mjs`) is the shipped benchmark
builder extended with a faculty dimension. It reproduces the original byte-for-byte at F = 1,
service = 1, sharedRooms = 0 — verified, because a generator that silently differs from the one whose
results are being compared against is worse than no generator.

### 2.2 The agreement gate

Every instance passes **Stage 0** before it is used: the C++ evaluator, run as `--mode score-hidden`,
must reproduce the independent JavaScript validator's nine Π counters and objective **to the digit**
on the planted schedule. Fifty archived instances plus every generated set pass.

This is what makes a quality number mean anything. The solver's own arithmetic is never trusted to
score the solver's own output: every run in the corpus is re-scored from scratch by
`validate.mjs`, which also reports the hard filters (`unplaced`, `badRoom`, `badBellSet`,
`constraintBreaches`, `duplicates`) independently.

### 2.3 Paired experimental design and the staged ladder

Every comparison is **paired on (instance, PRNG seed)**: arms differ only in flags, so the same seed
produces the same construction and the same operator draws until the flag first bites. Statistics are
non-parametric — exact sign test on wins/losses, Wilcoxon signed-rank on the paired differences,
Mann-Whitney U for unpaired instance-property comparisons — because the per-seed distribution of soft
is skewed and small-integer valued.

Arms are promoted through a ladder:

| stage | budget | workers | seeds | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 20–60 s | 1 | 1–2 | does it run, is it legal, does the mechanism fire at all |
| A | 60–180 s | 1, two runs in parallel | 6 | screening |
| B | 300 s | 1 | 6–20 | does the screen survive more seeds |
| C | 300–3 600 s | 2 | 3–18 | the shipped configuration; the only stage that may change a default |

**The ladder earns its cost.** Four separate mechanisms produced a convincing Stage A effect that
vanished at Stage B or C (§7). A campaign that promoted on screening would have shipped all four.

### 2.4 Correctness instruments

Two are worth naming because they generalise.

**Self-verifying optimisation modes.** When an optimisation replaces an exact computation with a
cheaper one that is claimed to be equivalent, a third mode computes *both* on every call and counts
disagreements. `--room-free-mask 3` did this for the room-occupancy mask: **1.61 × 10⁹ live queries
across three instances, zero mismatches.** That is a stronger guarantee than any unit test would have
given, and it costs one branch in a hot path plus a counter.

**Identity checks as a bug detector.** An arm that is *behaviour-identical by construction* should
tie its baseline on nearly every paired run. When `--room-building-first` returned soft values
identical to the baseline on four consecutive paired runs, that was not evidence it worked — a
reordering that shares an RNG stream should not agree exactly — and the flag turned out to be a
silent no-op. Conversely `--room-free-mask 1`, which *is* behaviour-identical, tied on 22 of 24
paired runs, exactly as designed. A flag that does nothing measures as a clean null; the tie pattern
is what distinguishes the two.

## 3. Claim R1

> **The residual вікна are a strict local optimum of the objective, not an unsearched region of the
> space. The endgame is limited by the model, not by the search.**

This is the campaign's central result and the one every later finding refers back to.

### 3.1 The evidence

A defect-focused exact repair was built (`STAGNATION-ESCAPE.md`): when the search has not improved
for a long time, enumerate the residual's defects and solve each exactly. For an (entity, day) slice
holding six movable classes, the day-slice solver enumerates up to **46 656 complete arrangements**,
including pulling a class in from another day, and returns the exact best. Run against defect after
defect on converged incumbents, it returns `best == before` — the incumbent is already optimal over
that whole neighbourhood.

### 3.2 The mechanism, and it is arithmetic

With Π₇ ≈ 26 lecturer windows and Π₈ ≈ 12 group windows, and α = 2:

- closing one lecturer window is worth β₇(26² − 25²) = 5 · 51 = **255**;
- the rearrangement that closes it typically opens one group window, costing
  β₈(12² − 11²) = 20 · 23 = **460**.

**Every single-defect repair is a trade that loses**, and it loses in both directions, because the
same arithmetic run the other way gives 460 gained against 255 lost. No procedure that requires each
step to improve can leave this configuration. The residual is an equilibrium.

### 3.3 What follows, and what was checked

Three consequences were tested and each held.

**Only a compound move can escape.** `forceCloseRepair` — which applies a multi-class rearrangement
as one atomic candidate against an explicit snapshot, rather than as a sequence of accepted moves —
is the only subsolver that ever cleared a defect, and it clears roughly 1 in 30.

**Flattening the objective converts the equilibrium into a plateau, and does not help.** Setting
α = 1 with equal comfort weights makes the exchange above a wash. Measured on `--soft-alpha 1
--soft-equal` against the shipped default:

| set | budget | workers | pairs | W/L/T | mean soft, off → on | relative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tight-XL n12800 | 300 s | 1 | 21 | 13/7/1 | 27.60 → 24.57 | −11.0 % |
| archived n12800 | 300 s | 1 | 20 | 15/5/0 | 15.25 → 12.20 | −20.0 % |
| archived n12800 | **900 s** | **2** | 6 | **2/4/0** | 7.83 → 8.00 | **+2.1 %** |

The composition is stable across all three: Π₇ falls by 2–4.6, Π₈ rises by 2–2.3, f(σ) is worse.
**It is a redistribution between lecturers and groups, not an improvement**, and the apparent gain at
the shorter single-worker budget does not survive the shipped configuration. Whether a deanery wants
that trade is a question about the model, and it belongs in the application's global properties, not
in the solver.

**An exact joint two-defect repair does not pay.** Solving two coupled defects simultaneously does
clear cases the single-defect ladder cannot (~1 in 30, like `forceCloseRepair`), but at 900 s on two
workers over eight paired seeds it costs more in candidates than the clearing is worth: median
17 → 18, mean Δ +2.50 (+14 %), W/L/T 2/4/2.

### 3.4 All three ways out have now been tried, and the barrier is in the hard terms

There are exactly three responses to "the incumbent is a local optimum of the objective", and the
campaign has now measured all three.

| | attack | mechanism | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | search further | exact day-slice and joint two-defect solvers | `best == before` over 4.7·10⁴ arrangements; the joint solver clears ~1 in 30 and costs more than it clears |
| 2 | change the objective | α = 1, equal comfort weights | redistributes Π₇ into Π₈; **+2.1 %** at the shipped configuration |
| 3 | change only the *walked* surface | guided local search (`--gls`) | **null**: 5/11, 7/8, 8/10 at λ = 2.5, 5, 10 over 18 paired runs each |

The GLS result is the informative one, because it is the classical answer to precisely this failure
mode and it was implemented faithfully — the penalty enters only the acceptance cost, the
late-acceptance history is refilled on every arm and disarm so the surface shift is not read as the
penalty's effect, and a counter confirms it armed 16–17 times per run. It still does nothing, and the
λ ladder trends *towards* neutral rather than through it (mean Δ +2.17 → +2.11 → +0.78), so "λ needs
tuning" does not survive.

**The explanation is that GLS changes the price of the destination and not the reachability of the
path, and only the second was ever the problem.** §3.2's arithmetic describes the *endpoints* of a
multi-class rearrangement. The search must still execute it as a sequence of individually accepted
candidates, and the intermediate states carry a **clash** — priced at λ_hard ≈ 10⁶, because moving
the first class into a slot the second has not yet vacated is a hard violation. A comfort-term
penalty of size 2.5–10 is irrelevant against a barrier of that height.

This retrospectively explains the one mechanism that *does* clear defects. `forceCloseRepair` applies
the whole rearrangement as **one atomic candidate against an explicit snapshot**, so the intermediate
states are never scored. The escape is not a pricing problem; it is a question of whether an operator
can express the whole exchange in a single move.

**Consequence for future work.** The remaining headroom in the endgame is an operator that proposes
multi-class exchanges atomically at scale — `forceCloseRepair`'s mechanism generalised beyond one
defect at a time — not a better penalty, not a wider neighbourhood search, and not a re-weighted
objective. That is a sharp, falsifiable direction, and it is the campaign's main recommendation.

### 3.5 Why this matters for a paper

The UCTP literature is dominated by search improvements. This result says that for a
quadratic-penalty objective at a good incumbent, the binding constraint can be the **penalty
function's local geometry** rather than the neighbourhood's reach — and it says so with an exhaustive
enumeration rather than an inference from stagnation. The arithmetic (255 against 460) is fully
determined by β and α and by the *current* values of Π₇ and Π₈, which means the trap is predictable
in advance from any incumbent: the exchange rate between two comfort terms under a power-α penalty is
βᵢ·α·Πᵢ^(α−1) against βⱼ·α·Πⱼ^(α−1), and a term that is already large is expensive to worsen and
cheap to improve. Any weighted-sum-of-powers objective has this property.

## 4. Claim R2

> **Of the instance properties that plausibly drive difficulty, exactly two are resolved: room
> capacity, and the share of classes that name no room at all. Room *eligibility* at fixed capacity
> is null, and cross-faculty coupling is at most a small effect.**

All four were measured on the same generator at n = 12 288, holding everything not under test fixed,
three instance seeds × three PRNG seeds per level.

### 4.1 Room capacity — resolved, large

| roomSlack | rooms | median soft | mean | p vs 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | 582 | 20.0 | 22.56 | — |
| 1.05 | 611 | 17.0 | 17.67 | 0.031 |
| 1.10 | 640 | 16.0 | 18.33 | 0.200 |
| 1.15 | 669 | 16.0 | 15.00 | 0.058 |
| 1.30 | 756 | 7.0 | 9.89 | **0.009** |

Thirty per cent more rooms more than halves the residual (−56 %). Pooled tight vs comfortable: 20.11
vs 12.44, p ≈ 0.012. Independently confirmed by pooling all coupling levels: `roomSlack 1.15`
(n = 24, mean 7.92) against `1.00` (n = 12, mean 13.33), Mann-Whitney **p ≈ 0.002**, same direction
in all four matched levels.

### 4.2 Room eligibility at fixed capacity — null

`--eligibility K` varies how many alternative rooms each class names, holding the room count (582),
the planted schedule and everything else identical.

| K | mean rooms named per class | mean soft | p vs K = 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.97 | 28.22 | — |
| 2 | 2.46 | 24.78 | 0.185 |
| 4 (shipped) | 3.44 | 24.33 | 0.270 |
| 8 | 5.39 | 26.50 | 0.564 |
| 16 | 9.30 | 22.89 | 0.102 |

Pooled narrow (K = 1, 2) against wide (K = 8, 16): 26.50 vs 24.59, **p ≈ 0.448**, non-monotone.

**Capacity is the constraint; assignment flexibility is not.** The mechanism is visible in how
alternatives are drawn — the same kind in the same building — so widening K redistributes demand
inside a pool whose size it cannot change. When a faculty's computer labs are all busy at 11:50,
naming nine of them instead of two does not create a tenth.

### 4.3 The share of classes naming *no* room — resolved, the largest effect measured

`--unrestricted-share u` sets the share of classes with empty `roomIds`, whose room domain is
therefore every room the faculty owns.

| level | classes naming no room | mean room domain | median soft | mean | Π₇ | Π₈ | Π₉ | mean moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ur0 | 12.6 % | 74.0 | 9 | 9.89 | 5.7 | 3.4 | 0.8 | 31.8 M |
| ur015 | 26.0 % | 150.3 | 23 | 26.11 | 15.2 | 10.0 | 0.9 | 25.4 M |
| ur035 | 43.2 % | 252.2 | 31 | 30.22 | 17.2 | 11.4 | 1.6 | 20.1 M |
| ur060 | 64.9 % | 377.2 | 37 | 38.67 | 20.2 | 15.1 | 3.3 | 15.8 M |
| ur10 | 100 % | 582.0 | 46 | 48.22 | 26.7 | 17.7 | 3.9 | 12.8 M |

Every column monotone. Mann-Whitney against ur0: p = 0.0006, 0.0013, 0.0004, 0.0003. Spearman across
all 45 runs against the measured share: **ρ = 0.800** (n = 45, t = 8.76).

**And it is not a difficulty result.** The planted schedule is byte-identical at every level (same
reference soft: 14 832 / 14 808 / 14 908 for the three instance seeds), and emptying `roomIds`
*removes* a constraint — every level is a relaxation of the one below. The solver is handed a
strictly larger feasible set and returns an answer five times worse. Availability-constraint size is
flat across levels (48.8–52.1 kB, non-monotone). The only monotone instance property is the mean room
domain, and the result tracks it. That makes this a **solver deficiency that scales with room-domain
size** — pursued in §5.

### 4.4 Cross-faculty coupling — not resolved

Composing a university from F faculties with a share of service teaching and shared rooms, at
n = 12 288: means 11.33 / 16.11 / 14.56 / 13.33 for service 0 / 5 / 20 / 100 %, Mann-Whitney against
0 % giving p ≈ 0.064, 0.354, 0.354. The per-instance means *within* the 20 % level run 7.7, 15.7,
20.3 — wider than the gap between levels. Coupling is at most a small effect and nine runs per level
cannot see it.

A methodological note worth keeping: an early attempt composed finished single-faculty instances and
added contention *post hoc*. That proved impossible at these densities — no room pair and no lecturer
pair across faculties has disjoint planted usage — so the one-pass generator was written instead.
Four rounds of composer bugs (entry-pair field shapes; classes occupying intervals rather than bells,
giving 24 planted conflicts; Π₅ travel being hard, giving 246 violations; fixed entries omitted from
the occupancy map) were each caught by the Stage 0 gate rather than by inspection.

### 4.5 The operational reading

Two of these compose into advice a deanery can act on, and it is sharper than it looks:

- Widening `roomIds` from one room to nine changes nothing (§4.2, p ≈ 0.448).
- Emptying `roomIds` entirely is catastrophic (§4.3, p ≈ 0.0003).

**Naming *some* rooms is what matters; how many is nearly irrelevant.** The share of curriculum items
left blank under «Призначення аудиторій» is, by a wide margin, the most consequential input property
measured — ahead of room slack, ahead of coupling, and ahead of every search-side arm tried. The
shipped benchmark sets are themselves at 22.7 % and 23.4 % blank, so this is not an exotic regime.

## 5. Claim R3

> **The room scan paid three separate O(bucket) costs per candidate room, none of which had to be
> paid per room. Removing them raises the candidate rate by 21–103 % and improves quality
> significantly where room domains are large — and not measurably where they are moderate.**

### 5.1 The three costs

| | what it was | why it did not have to be per room |
|---|---|---|
| **E7** | `roomClashesAt` walked the room's whole day to answer "is this room free?" | room buckets already carry unused occupancy-mask fields; the answer is one bitwise AND |
| **E8** | `placementAllowed` re-tested MAX_CLASSES_PER_DAY for the class's own lecturers and groups | that test depends on (class, day, parity) alone — the same answer for all 96 sampled rooms — and the whole slot fails when it fails |
| **E9** | the *room's* own per-day cap walked the room's day | per room by nature, so cached rather than hoisted: `BucketStat` gains two per-week counts, filled during a `recompute` that already walks the bucket |

E8 is the one worth dwelling on for a paper about engineering practice. `placementAllowed(i, day,
timeIdx, parity, room)` takes a room, so it was called inside the room loop; of its three tests only
two depend on `room`. The people-clash count and the window cost are already hoisted out of that
same loop for exactly this reason. **A predicate whose signature names a loop variable it only
partly depends on will be called once per iteration by whoever trusts the signature.**

All three are provably semantics-preserving, which makes the resulting experiment unusually clean:
the arms differ only in how many candidates the same search gets through, with nothing displaced and
no decision changed.

### 5.2 The sample size was on the wrong side of its optimum

Before the costs were removed, `roomSample` (96 rooms examined per slot when a domain exceeds 256)
was measured directly, 180 s, nine paired seeds per cell:

| rooms per slot | ur060 median | ur10 median | mean moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | **43** | **59** | 7.06 M / 6.12 M |
| 96 (shipped) | 61 | 76 | 4.91 M / 3.86 M |
| 256 | 56 | 106 | 2.93 M / 2.24 M |
| all 582 | 79 | 319 | 1.74 M / 1.42 M |

`--room-sample 24` wins 8 of 9 at both levels; mean Δ −14.33 and −15.44. Looking at *more* of a
class's options makes the answer worse, monotonically. Coverage was never the problem; paying for
coverage was.

**And removing the sample entirely is still wrong even after the costs are gone** (14/13, +4.33).
Six times as many rooms is six times the work however cheap each one becomes, and the marginal room
past a few dozen is almost never better than the best already seen. Sampling is correct; the shipped
size was not.

### 5.3 The composed result

Twenty-seven paired runs, 180 s, one worker, on `instances-unres`:

| arm | W/L/T | mean Δ soft | sign test | Wilcoxon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E8 + E9 alone | 15/10/2 | −7.41 | 0.424 | 0.058 |
| **E7 + E8 + E9** | **21/6/0** | **−9.00** | **0.0059** | **0.0198** |
| the same, unsampled | 14/13/0 | +4.33 | 1.000 | 0.494 |

Confirmed at **Stage C** — 900 s, two workers, 18 paired runs: **15/3, mean Δ −8.00, sign test
p = 0.0075**, with medians 29 → 20 (ur060) and 34 → 29 (ur10) and candidate rates up 84–103 %. This
is the only mechanism in the campaign to survive Stage C.

Note which row is *not* significant: E8 + E9 alone is 15/10 at p = 0.42. The day-cap hoist matters
only as one of three, and reported alone would have read as another null. The decomposition is kept
so a later reader knows which part to keep if one must be reverted.

### 5.4 Where it does not transfer, and the rule that failed

On the sets that decide defaults — archived-style, two workers, 300 s, 18 paired runs per arm — every
arm is a coin: `--room-sample 24` 9/8/1 (−0.17), the cheap scan 9/8/1 (+0.11), both 9/7/2 (−0.28),
with candidate rates up 21–43 %.

A rule was proposed to reconcile this: that the variable is how far the residual still is from the
§3 equilibrium when the clock stops, since the wins occurred at residual 20–80 and the null at 8–22.
It fitted every point then available. **E13 falsified it.** Holding the instance family fixed and
moving only the budget on tight-XL:

| budget | residual | cheap scan | with sample 24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 s | 30.5 | 8/7/3, −0.50, p = 1.000 | 8/10, +0.22 |
| 180 s | 23 | 11/6, −2.06, p = 0.332 | 7/8, −1.06 |
| 300 s | 22 | 3/5, +1.44 | 4/3, −1.67 |

No rung wins, at a residual of 30 where the rule predicted one, and the ordering is not monotone.

**What survives is narrower and is two claims at different confidences:**

| | mean room domain | throughput gained | Δ soft | resolved? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `instances-unres` | 377–582 | +45–103 % | −8.00 to −9.00 | **yes**, p ≈ 0.006–0.008, at two budgets and two worker counts |
| tight-XL / uni1 | 134–159 | +25–33 % | −2.06 to +1.44 | **no** — signs flip between sets, budgets and arms at up to 18 pairs |

The predictor is the **room domain**, and through it how much throughput the change actually buys.
Not the residual, and not the budget.

### 5.5 Verdict

**No defaults change.** All four flags (`--room-sample`, `--room-free-mask`, `--hoist-day-caps`,
`--cap-cache`) stay defaulted to the shipped behaviour, with these numbers recorded beside them.
They are worth enabling where classes name few or no rooms; on this evidence they are not worth
enabling elsewhere.

## 6. Claim R4

> **Fourteen mechanisms were implemented and measured; thirteen are negative, and most fail for one
> of three reasons — no spare draws, no gradient left to buy, or no path across the hard-term
> barrier.**

### 6.1 The catalogue

| mechanism | flag | result |
|---|---|---|
| exhaustive (entity, day) re-pack | `--dayfix` | 20 → 27 at n = 12 800 |
| window-directed move | `--winfix` | 20 → 26 |
| budget-proportional operator selection | `--cost-aware` | 20 → 31, with double the candidates |
| widened permutation pool | `--kopt-extra 8` | 5 → 11 at n = 6 400 |
| cluster recombination | `--recombine` | mean 20.2 → 23.3 |
| dedicated ILS loop | `--ils` | 37 → 70 when early |
| longer LAHC history | `--lahc 5000` | does not reach feasibility at 60 s |
| regret-2 repair | `--repair-order 2` | 2.5× the move cost for no quality |
| most-constrained-first repair | `--repair-order 1` | wins at n = 3 200 (−21 %), nowhere above it |
| flattened escape phase | `--flat-phase` | −8 % at 6 seeds → null at 20 |
| construction tournament | `--construct-tournament` | worse on 5 of 6 seeds |
| stuck-state machinery (E2/E4) | `--hard-repair` | neutral at the hour; ~2 % of the move rate |
| exact joint two-defect repair | `--no-joint-repair` | median 17 → 18, W/L/T 2/4/2 |
| guided local search on the residual | `--gls X` | null at λ = 2.5/5/10: 5/11, 7/8, 8/10 over 18 pairs each |

### 6.2 Reason one — a portfolio with large neighbourhoods has no spare draws

`repack` and `ruin` produce ~79 % of the total gain from 43 % of the draws (STUDY.md §4). Any new operator placed
in the bandit takes draws from them, and the arithmetic almost never works out. `dayfix`, `winfix`
and `recombine` all died this way, and so did the Stage B combination of four new deep-phase
operators: moving them out of the bandit and into the deep phase did not save them, because the deep
phase's 24 attempts were then split five or six ways instead of three, and the three they were split
from were the ones paying.

The corollary is a placement rule rather than a prohibition: a cheap mechanism matched to the
residual belongs **after** the proven operators have come back empty, not beside them.

### 6.3 Reason two — throughput does not buy the endgame

`--cost-aware` more than doubles the candidate count and loses. `--simple` makes ten times the
candidates and is fifteen times worse (STUDY.md §4). §5.4's scan fixes raise the candidate rate 21–43 % on the
shipped sets and move nothing.

These are not in tension. `--simple` removes the operators that *create* the gradient, so its
throughput has nothing to descend. `--cost-aware` divides a large neighbourhood's worth by the work
it costs, and a large neighbourhood's worth is not its immediate gain per bucket touched — it is
reaching states the cheap moves cannot reach at all, which a rate divides away. And on an incumbent
at the §3 equilibrium there is no downhill for extra candidates to find.

### 6.4 Reason three — the barrier is in the hard terms

Guided local search is the one negative that fails for neither of the above reasons: it takes no
draws from anything (it changes a cost, not a portfolio) and it costs no throughput (the candidate
rate is marginally *higher* than the baseline's). It fails because the multi-class rearrangement it
makes attractive still has to be walked through intermediate states that carry a clash at
λ_hard ≈ 10⁶, and no comfort-term penalty of a plausible size crosses that. See §3.4.

The rule this yields: **a mechanism that reprices endpoints cannot help when the barrier is in the
path.** Before building one, check whether the intermediate states of the exchange you want are
feasible; if they are not, the mechanism needed is an atomic multi-class operator, not a price.

### 6.5 One positive result worth separating

`--actionable-windows` is a **fix to the shipped search**, found while diagnosing why the hard
repair's exact subsolvers were winning 0 of 42 attempts. `State::worstWindowBuckets` ranks
(entity, day) buckets by window count, and that list is read by the `worstWindow` ruin selector, by
the permutation's member draw, by the kick and by `opCloseWindow`. Buckets whose вікна are held open
by **immovable** classes — the fixed entries of other faculties this run schedules around — rise to
the top of that list *precisely because* nothing has been able to tidy them, and no draw aimed at one
can close anything. Six of six enumerated defects had ≤ 1 movable member.

The general form is worth stating: **a list ranked by "how bad is this" will be dominated by items
that are bad because they cannot be fixed.** Any mechanism that targets the worst of something needs
a movability filter, or it aims all of its effort at the immovable.

## 7. Claim R5

> **Every effect this campaign measured shrank or vanished as the sample grew. This is the most
> transferable finding in the file.**

### 7.1 The pattern

| effect | at first measurement | at fuller measurement |
|---|---|---|
| flattened escape phase | −8 % at 6 seeds | null at 20 seeds (mean −0.95, W/L/T 10/9/1) |
| objective shape (α = 1, equal weights) | −19.1 % at 300 s, 1 worker | **+2.1 %** at 900 s, 2 workers |
| cross-faculty coupling | "triples the residual" at 3 seeds | not resolved at 9 seeds |
| room-scan fixes | −9.00 at Stage A | **−8.00 at Stage C** — the exception |
| the residual rule (§5.4) | fitted 3 points | falsified by the first experiment designed to test it |

Four of five shrank. One survived. That ratio is the argument for the ladder.

### 7.2 The variance that causes it

Same binary, same instance, six seeds: soft 11–29, a factor of 2.6 (STUDY.md §6a); twelve seeds at
180 s span a factor of 3.2 (STUDY.md §9). On one archived instance the six values were 16, 19, 7, 23, 9, 14. **Six paired seeds
resolve a ~20 % effect with power 0.34** (STUDY.md §1). Most published-looking effects at six seeds are inside that
band.

### 7.3 The structural tell

The residual rule (§5.4) is instructive because it was killed by design rather than by more data.
Its shape was: **three data points, each differing in two variables, fitted by a story about one of
them.** When that shape appears, the next experiment is not more seeds on the same comparison — it is
the one that moves a single variable, even when it costs hours and the existing points look
convincing.

### 7.4 A pitfall specific to flag-guarded optimisation

A flag that silently does nothing measures as a clean null and is indistinguishable from a mechanism
that was fairly tested and lost. Two habits catch it: a **counter for how many times the mechanism
fired** (a run with zero armings tested nothing, whatever the flag said), and attention to
**exact ties** — arms sharing an RNG stream and differing only in a reordering should not agree to
the digit.

## 8. Threats to validity

Stated plainly, because several of them bound the claims above.

**Generated instances, not real curricula.** Every number here comes from a generator whose planted
schedule is known to be perfect. Real faculty data may differ in structure — correlations between
lecturer availability and group structure, for instance — that the generator does not reproduce. The
instance-property findings (§4) are the ones most exposed to this: they say what happens as *this
generator's* parameters move.

**Two cores.** All screening ran with one worker and two runs in parallel; Stage C used two workers.
The cooperation mechanism between workers is exercised only at Stage C, and portfolio effects at 8 or
16 workers are entirely unmeasured. At least one negative (`--recombine`) is explicitly suspected to
be a small-portfolio artefact.

**One instance family per claim, mostly.** §4's ladders each vary one parameter of one generator
configuration. The room-slack result is confirmed across four coupling levels; the others are not
cross-confirmed.

**Budgets to one hour.** The trajectory at n = 12 288 is still descending at the half-hour (51 → 17
over an hour). Nothing here says what happens at eight hours, and the escape ladder's constants were
tuned at a budget where `restartAfter` = 1.2 M moves barely fires.

**Statistical multiplicity.** Roughly thirty arm-vs-baseline comparisons were made without a
family-wise correction. The two results reported as significant (§4.3 at p ≈ 0.0003, §5.3 at
p ≈ 0.006–0.0075) survive a Bonferroni correction at 30 comparisons; nothing else claimed here is
reported as significant.

**The residual is small and integer-valued.** At soft ≈ 8–22 on n = 12 288, a difference of one
window is 5–12 % of the metric. Medians move in steps that means do not, and the two disagree in
several tables above. Both are reported for that reason.

## 9. What could be written up

Four candidate contributions, in descending order of how well the evidence supports them.

### 9.1 The equilibrium result (§3) — the strongest

**Claim.** For a UCTP objective that is a weighted sum of powers of penalty counts, a converged
incumbent can be a strict local optimum over neighbourhoods of size 4·10⁴ *because of the penalty
geometry*, and this is provable from β, α and the current Π values rather than inferred from
stagnation.

**What makes it publishable.** Exhaustive enumeration over complete day-slice arrangements, including
cross-day pullers, returning `best == before` defect after defect — not "the search stopped
improving" but "there is nothing there". Plus the closed-form exchange rate βᵢ·α·Πᵢ^(α−1), which
predicts the trap in advance, and the negative result that flattening the objective *redistributes*
between lecturers and groups rather than reducing total windows.

**Related work to position against.** ITC-2007/2019 objective formulations and the literature on
penalty weighting in course timetabling. Guided local search and the breakout method (Voudouris &
Tsang) are the classical response to exactly this failure mode — and this campaign implemented GLS
and measured it null (§3.4), with a mechanistic explanation for why: the barrier separating the
incumbent from a better arrangement lives in the *hard* terms traversed mid-rearrangement, not in
the comfort terms GLS reprices. That negative, with its diagnosis, is arguably more interesting than
the positive result would have been.

### 9.2 The instance-property study (§4) — the most useful to practitioners

**Claim.** Under controlled variation with the planted optimum held identical, room *capacity* and
the share of classes naming *no* room dominate difficulty; room *eligibility* at fixed capacity does
not.

**What makes it publishable.** Backwards generation makes it a genuine controlled experiment rather
than a correlation across a benchmark library: at every level of every ladder the same schedule is
optimal and scores the same. The eligibility null next to the capacity effect is the interesting
pair, and the unrestricted-share result has the striking property that **relaxing a constraint makes
the solver five times worse** — a solver-scaling statement, not a difficulty statement.

**Practitioner payoff.** "Name a few rooms per class; naming more buys nothing" is actionable,
cheap, and counter to the intuition that more flexibility must help.

### 9.3 The predicate-hoisting result (§5.1–5.3) — the most reusable engineering finding

**Claim.** In an incremental-evaluation local search, per-candidate cost can hide inside a predicate
whose signature suggests a dependency it does not have, and the resulting waste scales with a domain
size the solver's author never thought of as a parameter.

**What makes it publishable.** It is a concrete, measured instance of a general pattern, with a
correctness methodology attached (the self-verifying mode, 1.61 G queries, zero mismatches) and an
honest negative half (it does not transfer to moderate domains).

### 9.4 The methodology (§2, §7) — the most transferable

**Claim.** Backwards instance generation plus an independent-validator agreement gate plus a paired
staged ladder is a design under which negative results are trustworthy, and four of five apparently
significant screening effects do not survive it.

**What makes it publishable.** Metaheuristic papers report wins. A quantified account of how often a
six-seed screening effect survives a 20-seed or Stage C re-measurement — four of five did not — is
the kind of thing the field knows anecdotally and rarely measures. The falsified-rule episode (§5.4,
§7.3) is a worked example with the diagnosis attached.

## 10. What is still open

### 10.1 Never implemented

| idea | why it is still interesting |
|---|---|
| **E6** exact permutation with branch-and-bound | `kopt` has the best hit rate of any operator (13.65 % improving) and one draw in a thousand; its known weakness is that it is exact only over a relaxation that ignores member–member coupling |
| **T2.4** path relinking | the diverse-recombination idea that `--recombine` failed as; a different operator, not a retry |
| **T2.6** escape-level bandit | the escape ladder's three levels are chosen by fixed move counts and nothing adapts them to observed payoff |
| **T2.7** diverse adoption | workers adopt the best elite; adopting a *distant* one is implemented (`--switch-policy 2`) but the diversity criterion is unmeasured |
| **T2.10** ruin-size adaptation | `lnsMin`/`lnsMax` are fixed at 6/40 and never adapted |

### 10.2 Implemented, unmeasured

- `--restart-adaptive` (E5) — restart on observed basin ripening time rather than a fixed count.
- `--engine dlas` / `mixed` (T2.8) — every measurement in the study is on LAHC.
- `--room-building-first`, `--room-travel-budget` — the last O(bucket) cost in the room loop is
  `travelCostOfAdding`, budgeted to six rooms per slot, so ninety of ninety-six sampled rooms are
  scored as though travel were free. Expect the shape of §5.4: real where domains are large,
  unresolved where they matter.
T2.1 guided local search is no longer open: implemented, measured, null (§3.4; `CAMPAIGN-LOG.md`
§6r).

### 10.3 The one direction §3.4 points at

**An atomic multi-class exchange operator.** `forceCloseRepair` is the only mechanism that has ever
cleared a defect, and it works because it applies a whole rearrangement as one candidate against a
snapshot, so the clash-carrying intermediate states are never scored. It currently handles one defect
at a time, found by the stuck-state ladder, and clears roughly 1 in 30.

Generalising it is the campaign's clearest recommendation: an operator that can propose a k-class
cyclic exchange atomically, at bandit frequency rather than only at stagnation, and whose legality
test runs once on the completed set. The pieces exist — the journal supports compound candidates, the
snapshot mechanism is written, and `allLegal` already re-verifies a full set. What is missing is a
*selector*: which k classes form an exchange worth attempting. §3.2's arithmetic gives a criterion —
look for a cycle whose net Σ βᵢ(Πᵢ² − (Πᵢ−1)²) is negative — which is computable from the bucket
statistics without trying the move.

Note the interaction with rule 5 (§6.2): this is a cheap operator and must therefore live in an
escalation phase, not in the bandit portfolio, or it will lose by displacing `repack`.

### 10.4 The largest lever, still untouched

**The basin lottery.** Same binary, same instance, twelve seeds at 180 s span a factor of 3.2 in
final quality. The restart machinery *samples* that distribution; nothing **steers** it. Every
construction is independent of everything every previous basin learned. Two facts sharpen this:
building three constructions and descending the best of them is *worse* than descending one
(`--construct-tournament`, 5 of 6 seeds), so sampling the distribution more often beats sampling it
more selectively; and anti-backbone restarts, which is the obvious way to steer, are implemented
(`--switch-policy 1`) but were measured only as part of the larger stuck-state combination.

Given §3, this is where the remaining headroom most plausibly is: if the endgame is an equilibrium
the search cannot leave, then which equilibrium it lands in is the thing worth controlling.

### 10.5 The one question a real dataset would answer

Every §4 result is a statement about generated instances. A single run of the shipped solver on a
real LNU faculty's data, reporting the residual and the share of curriculum items with empty
`roomIds`, would place that faculty on §4.3's curve and say immediately whether the room-domain
finding applies to it. That is a two-minute check with an actionable answer, and it is the highest
value-per-effort item on this list.
